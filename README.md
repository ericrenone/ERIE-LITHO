## Part II · The Serpentinization-Driven Lost-City-and-Kunlun Hydrogen Federation

### II.1 Thought Experiment: The Towers That Make Hydrogen From Mountains

Imagine the Mid-Atlantic Ridge — the slow-spreading center where the North American and Eurasian plates are pulling apart at approximately 2.5 cm per year. Mid-ridge magmatism creates fresh basaltic crust at the axis. But in slow-spreading systems, the magmatic supply is intermittent and the brittle deformation produces detachment faults — long-lived normal faults that exhume mantle peridotite directly onto the seafloor. At the Atlantis Massif, 30°N on the MAR, one such detachment exposes a domal core complex composed almost entirely of olivine-rich peridotite, the same mineralogy as Earth's upper mantle.

Now imagine seawater seeping into the fractures of this exposed mantle rock. At 100–200 °C, in the absence of free oxygen, olivine reacts with water in the canonical serpentinization reaction:

$$
\text{(Mg,Fe)}_2\text{SiO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Mg}_3\text{Si}_2\text{O}_5(\text{OH})_4 + \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + \text{H}_2
$$

The rock — formerly mantle peridotite — transforms to serpentine plus magnetite plus molecular hydrogen. The hydrogen accumulates in the fluid; the magnetite is locked into the rock; the serpentine is a soft, slick, hydrated phyllosilicate. Where the alkaline (pH 9–11), hydrogen-rich (up to 15 mmol/kg) fluid emerges at the seafloor and mixes with cold ocean water, calcium carbonate plus brucite precipitate as towering chimneys up to 60 meters tall. This is the Lost City Hydrothermal Field, discovered in December 2000 (Kelley et al. *Nature* 2001; *Science* 2005). The field has been operational for at least 30,000 years and possibly 100,000 years.

The fluids contain methane, formate, and trace hydrocarbons of confirmed abiotic origin. The pH-9-to-11 conditions favor RNA-bearing vesicle stability. The hydrogen plus carbon dioxide redox couple represents one of the deepest chemical disequilibria known on Earth. The alkaline-hydrothermal-vent origin-of-life hypothesis — Russell, Martin, Nick Lane, Sojo, Williams — proposes that the porous, mineralized, naturally-compartmentalized inorganic chimneys of a Hadean Lost-City-class vent system provided the substrate on which the first protometabolic networks evolved, four billion years ago.

In June 2025, Chinese researchers using the *Fendouzhe* human-occupied submersible reported the discovery of the Kunlun Hydrothermal Field near the Mussau Trench in the western Pacific. The field is more than one hundred times larger than Lost City: twenty round craters of hundreds of meters in diameter, total area 11.1 km², dolomite-and-calcite carbonate deposits up to 4 m above the seafloor. Hydrogen flux: 4.8 × 10¹¹ moles per year, contributing more than 5% of the global submarine abiotic hydrogen flux. The size and flux of Kunlun reset the inventory of natural hydrogen production by nearly an order of magnitude.
